# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People doing a short home workout in front of a laptop or TV, usually standing one to three metres from the screen. They need large, peripheral, movement-first feedback while jumping, squatting, and reaching; desk controls remain a useful fallback for demos and accessibility.

## Product Purpose

THE HERD turns a three-minute interval workout into a responsive lane runner controlled by the player's body. Success means movement is recognized quickly, hazards are readable while the player is in motion, effort feels rewarded, and a complete run feels energetic rather than punishing.

## Brand Personality

Bright, kinetic, encouraging. The product should feel like a sunny outdoor training session crossed with an arcade runner: playful enough to invite another round, disciplined enough to remain legible at a high heart rate.

## Anti-references

The current violet dusk treatment, horror-game tension, muddy low-contrast scenery, generic dark fitness dashboards, and decorative sci-fi HUD chrome. Avoid any purple-led palette or presentation that makes exercise feel ominous.

## Design Principles

- Movement is the interface: every important state must be legible without reading a paragraph.
- Respond before explaining: avatar motion and immediate feedback confirm an input within the next rendered frame.
- Celebrate effort: color, scale, and motion should make successful movement feel rewarding.
- Keep the workout honest: rewards come from movement and recovery moments never incentivize coasting.
- Spend performance on play: visual richness must yield to pose responsiveness when the device is under load.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for interface text and controls. Never communicate an obstacle or game state by color alone. Support keyboard play, visible focus, reduced-motion preferences, large room-readable type, and pause-without-penalty when tracking is lost.
